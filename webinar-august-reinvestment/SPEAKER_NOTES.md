### Slide 1 — Title (1 min)
Open with the month, not the product. “This is the largest domestic redemption month of the quarter. If you hold government paper, cash is about to hit your account. The only question is whether it is still invested on Friday.”

### Slide 2 — Agenda (1 min)
Promise four things and keep them: calendar, compounding, two funds, this week’s actions. Tell people they can sit with a DhowCSD printout.

### Slide 3 — Why August (3 min)
- **KES 294bn** vs **KES 152bn** in July (SIB / CBK). That is the hook.
- 17 August is the concentrated date.
- Even investors who are not “in a maturing bond” may be receiving coupons.

### Slide 4 — Bonds maturing (4 min)
Walk the table slowly.

- **FXD1/2016/010** — 10-year, 15.039% coupon, **full maturity 17 August 2026**. This is the poster child: a high-coupon paper disappearing.
- **IFB1/2020/011** — 10.90%, tax-free. On CBK’s 17 Aug redemption calendar. Original tenor was 11 years to 2031; August 2026 is the typical year-6 amortisation window. Confirm each client’s holding in DhowCSD rather than assuming 100% principal.
- **T-bills** — 17 / 24 / 31 August. Easy to ignore because they feel “small and frequent.”
- **Switch** — FXD1/2012/015 + three T-bills into **FXD4/2019/010**, bids **24 Aug 10:00**, settlement **26 Aug**. Optional. Don’t pressure; explain duration extension vs cash.

### Slide 5 — Coupons (3 min)
Use a concrete example: KES 2m face of FXD1/2021/020 at 13.444% is about **KES 134,440 a year**, paid in two instalments. One August coupon is ~KES 67,000. “That is a weekend in Diani, or a year of compounding. It will not feel like a decision unless we name it.”

### Slide 6 — The leak (3 min)
Empathy, not scolding. School fees are real. The point is: **pay fees from a budgeted MMF sleeve, not by accident from a matured 10-year bond.** Inflation at 6.5% (July 2026) is the silent tax on idle cash. CBR is 8.75%.

### Slide 7 — Compounding chart (5 min)
Stay on this slide. Trace four lines:

1. Spend — the line dies in year 1.
2. 2% account — looks “safe,” barely outruns nothing.
3. MMF 9% — KES 1m → **2.37m** in 10 years.
4. FIF 12% — KES 1m → **3.11m** in 10 years.

Say twice: **illustrative, not a guarantee.** Industry MMF average has been around 9% gross; fixed income funds around 12% gross in mid-2026. Clients will not all get the top fund.

### Slide 8 — Table (3 min)
The 15-year FIF number (**KES 5.47m**) is the one people remember. Contrast with spending = zero. Ask: “Which of these is the plan for the money landing on 17 August?”

### Slide 9 — Why reinvest (3 min)

### Slide 11 — Case for MMF (4 min)
Best line: **“The MMF is the default so that indecision still pays.”** Standing order from the linked bank into the fund. Mention CMA, custodian, trustee. Do not name a fund unless you are licensed to sell it.

### Slide 12 — Case for Fixed Income (4 min)
Best line: **“A 10-year bond should not retire into a 91-day bill by accident.”** New 10-year FXD accepted around **12.78%** (13 July 2026 auction, FXD1/2022/010). IFB reopenings on 17 August cleared about **12.2–13.1%** tax-free — useful colour, not a call to chase the last auction.

Risk honesty: NAV can wobble if yields rise. That is why the holding period is 12 months+.

### Slide 16 — Close / Q&A (10 min)
Invite DhowCSD screenshots (holdings only). Map each line to MMF, FIF, or a dated withdrawal. Read the disclaimer once, calmly.

**Likely questions**

- *“Can I roll into the new IFB?”* Yes, if they already bid; settlement was 17 Aug. If they missed it, the fund is the practical path.
- *“Is 15% coming back?”* Unlikely near-term with CBR at 8.75%. Don’t promise a coupon.
- *“Are MMFs safe?”* CMA-licensed, custodian held, not a bank deposit, not a CDSC guarantee of yield. No fund is zero risk.
- *“What about buying another FXD myself?”* Fine for investors who want a single name. Funds for everyone else.
